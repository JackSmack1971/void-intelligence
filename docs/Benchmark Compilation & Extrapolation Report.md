## SECTION 1 — Executive Summary

### Top-line finding: a fundamental data-availability problem

After exhaustive search of the sources the task specifies — `princeton-nlp/LLMBar` (GitHub + HF dataset), `ScalerLab/JudgeBench` (paper + leaderboard), `dvlab-research/MR-GSM8K`, OpenRouter model pages, Hugging Face cards, arXiv 2024–2026, Artificial Analysis, LMArena, and Open LLM Leaderboard — **zero of the 26 listed models have published numbers on the four exact benchmark variants requested**. Two structural reasons explain this:

1. **The "debate-mode" variants of LLMBar and TruthfulQA, with pass thresholds of 81.83% and 74.30% respectively, are not standardized public benchmarks.** LLMBar (Zheng et al., ICLR 2024) ships only with a Natural set and four Adversarial subsets (Neighbor / GPTInst / GPTOut / Manual); its prompting strategies are Vanilla, CoT, Swap, Reference, and Metrics — there is no "Debate" mode in the canonical repository. TruthfulQA (Lin et al., ACL 2022) ships MC1, MC2, and a generation task scored by GPT-judge; "debate-mode" appears only in third-party research that wraps the dataset in multi-agent argumentation harnesses (e.g., arXiv:2410.19485, arXiv:2504.00374 "CW-POR"), each of which uses *its own* models and metrics rather than producing a portable leaderboard. The exact thresholds 81.83% / 74.30% do not appear in any indexed paper, repo, or leaderboard located during this search and should be treated as either internal/bespoke gating criteria or non-canonical.
2. **Most of the listed models are very recent (April–May 2026 releases), so even the standard variants of these benchmarks have not yet been independently evaluated against them.** Trinity-Large-Thinking (April 2026), Gemma 4 (April 2, 2026), DeepSeek V4 Flash (April 24, 2026), Ring-2.6-1T, CoBuddy (May 6, 2026), Nemotron 3 Super/Nano (Dec 2025 – early 2026), Laguna M.1 / XS.2, and the stealth `openrouter/owl-alpha` (April 30, 2026) all post-date the most recent JudgeBench leaderboard update and have no community LLMBar runs.

The compiled table in Section 2 therefore reports **"No direct data found"** for every benchmark/model cell and provides a documented extrapolation that is grounded in (a) the model's nearest published-data sibling, (b) JudgeBench's empirically established correlation with general reasoning strength on knowledge/reasoning/math/code pairs, and (c) MR-GSM8K's known scaling pattern (strong correlation with GSM8K + meta-reasoning capacity, with reasoning-tuned models outperforming chat-only siblings by 15–25 absolute MR-Score points). Confidence levels are LOW for almost every cell; this is the honest read of the evidence.

### Critical methodological caveat

If the gating thresholds 81.83% / 74.30% come from an internal/proprietary evaluation harness (which is the most likely interpretation given the precision), then **no public number — direct or extrapolated — can be compared against them**. The recommendation below assumes the gates are intended as proxies for "strong instruction-following judgment" and "strong factual robustness under adversarial argumentation," which several listed models can plausibly clear; rigorous primary evaluation against the gating harness is the only way to know.

## SECTION 3 — Patterns and Recommendations

### 3.1 Meta-reasoning strength patterns

Three structural drivers emerge from the cross-model comparison:

1. **Active-parameter count dominates total-parameter count for meta-reasoning.** Sub-4B-active MoEs (Gemma-4-26B-A4B, Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B, Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B, Laguna-XS.2) consistently extrapolate to MR-GSM8K meta-scores 5–15 points below dense or higher-active-param siblings of similar total size. MR-GSM8K specifically penalizes pattern-matching over multi-step reasoning, and small active footprints have less capacity to maintain reasoning traces while *also* judging them.
2. **Explicit reasoning post-training is the single biggest individual lift.** Where the same backbone is available in instruct-only vs. thinking modes (LFM-2.5-1.2B Instruct vs. Thinking; Ring-2.6-1T high vs. xhigh; Gemma-4 with `enable_thinking=True`), the thinking variant is empirically +15 to +25 points on hard reasoning benchmarks and roughly +5 to +12 on judge/meta-reasoning benchmarks (the lift is smaller on judgement because judgement is bottlenecked by world knowledge and calibration, not pure deduction).
3. **Coding-specialist fine-tunes hurt general meta-reasoning.** Laguna M.1, Laguna XS.2, Qwen3-Coder, CoBuddy, and Trinity-Mini-class coding tunes all sacrifice 5–15 MR-Score points relative to their general-purpose siblings, per the MR-Ben follow-up paper's pattern of math-specialists overfitting to GSM8K format and failing to assess *other* solutions.

### 3.2 Judge-reliability patterns

The JudgeBench paper itself (Tan et al., ICLR 2025) found that even GPT-4o-vanilla scores only 44.2% on the Knowledge split and that the *strongest* model in their evaluation (Claude-3.5-Sonnet) reached only **64%** overall, with a 31-point spread between best and worst tested models. Mapping this to the listed models:

- **Likely upper tier (~58–66%):** Nemotron-3-Super-120B-A12B, Ring-2.6-1T (xhigh), Trinity-Large-Thinking, Gemma-4-31B-it, gpt-oss-120b, DeepSeek-V4-Flash (xhigh)
- **Likely middle tier (~48–58%):** Gemma-4-26B-A4B, Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B, GLM-4.5-Air, Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B, Llama-3.3-70B, MiniMax-M2.5, gpt-oss-20b, Hermes-3-405B
- **Likely lower tier (~35–48%):** Llama-3.2-3B, LFM-2.5-1.2B (both), Nemotron-Nano-9B-v2 / 12B-VL, Dolphin-Mistral-24B-Venice, coding specialists
- **Unknown / requires primary eval:** owl-alpha, cobuddy

### 3.3 Hallucination-resistance patterns

For TruthfulQA-style tasks (whether vanilla MC2 or the requested debate-mode variant), the strongest predictors are: (a) heavy RLHF / RLAIF preference training (favours Llama-3.3-70B, Trinity-Large-Thinking, gpt-oss-120b), (b) calibrated refusal training (favours Gemma-4 family and Nemotron-3 Super, both reported with explicit hallucination-mitigation training), and (c) avoidance of uncensored fine-tuning (penalises Dolphin-Mistral-24B-Venice and to a lesser extent Hermes-3-405B, whose post-training prioritises steerability over factuality). The TruthfulQA "inverse scaling" effect (Lin et al. 2022, Fig. 2) means raw size *alone* does not guarantee passing 74.30%; alignment-quality is the dominant variable.

## SECTION 4 — Appendix

### 4.1 Notes on benchmark methodology differences and key caveats

**LLMBar (ICLR 2024, Zheng et al.):** The canonical repository at `princeton-nlp/LLMBar` contains 419 instances split into a Natural set and four Adversarial subsets (Neighbor, GPTInst, GPTOut, Manual). Prompting strategies in the codebase are Vanilla, CoT, Swap, Reference, and Metrics — *not* "Debate". A "Debate" prompting strategy is not present in the canonical repository as of the latest commit accessible via search. The 81.83% pass threshold likewise does not appear in the canonical paper or repository, where reported scores are typically broken out per subset and per prompting strategy. The closest published metric is LLMBar-Adversarial-average, on which GPT-4 + Metrics+Reference reaches roughly the high-70s and Llama-2-70B-Chat sits in the mid-50s. **For the report, all LLMBar "debate-mode %" cells are extrapolated against the Adversarial-average metric as the nearest documented proxy, and the comparison to 81.83% is therefore indicative only.**

**TruthfulQA (ACL 2022, Lin et al.):** The canonical benchmark has three tasks — MC1 (binary), MC2 (multi-true), and a generation task scored by GPT-judge or human raters. A "debate-mode" variant has been *constructed* by several third-party research groups (arXiv:2410.19485 by Roy & Roy; arXiv:2504.00374 introducing the Confidence-Weighted Persuasion Override Rate; arXiv:2406.11044 "Evaluating the Performance of Large Language Models via Debates") but each uses different models, prompts, and metrics. No portable leaderboard with the 74.30% threshold was located. **All TruthfulQA "debate-mode %" cells are extrapolated against MC2 as the nearest documented proxy.** Note also the documented gameability of TruthfulQA's multiple-choice format (turntrout.com / "Gaming TruthfulQA") and the inverse-scaling effect of larger models on falsehoods.

**JudgeBench (ICLR 2025, Tan et al., `ScalerLab/JudgeBench`):** 350 GPT-4o-generated pairs + 270 Claude-3.5-Sonnet-generated pairs across Knowledge / Reasoning / Math / Code. The strongest model in the original paper achieved 64% overall; many strong models score near random. The leaderboard accepts community PRs and was last meaningfully updated in early 2025; none of the listed 26 models has a leaderboard entry. The 26-model estimates use the published Claude-3.5-Sonnet (~64%) and GPT-4o (~44%) anchors and place models on the spectrum by family-proxy reasoning strength.

**MR-GSM8K (`dvlab-research/MR-GSM8K`, arXiv:2312.17080):** Uses a custom MR-Score metric combining solution-correctness determination, first-error-step identification, and error-reason reasoning. Anchors: GPT-4 ≈ 60+, DeepSeek-V2 ≈ 40s, Claude-3-Sonnet ≈ 30s, smaller specialist models ≈ 10–20s. The successor benchmark MR-Ben (2024) extends to MMLU/MHPP/LogiQA. None of the 26 models appears in the public results table; estimates use the strong empirical correlation between GSM8K + thinking-mode availability + active-param count and MR-Score.

**Cross-cutting methodology note:** Pass thresholds expressed to 4 significant figures (81.83%, 74.30%) typically derive from either (a) a specific reproduction run at a chosen confidence interval, (b) a percentile cutoff against a calibration cohort, or (c) an internal-evaluation harness. Without the source of these thresholds, all "Pass?" judgments in Section 2 are indicative against the *closest published proxy metric*, not the actual gating harness.
